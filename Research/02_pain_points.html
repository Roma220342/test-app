WONDERLINE · LOD — Where to shoot

The one pain point that wins the trial

Everything the client cares about collides on a single screen. Hit it and the free trial converts into a longer engagement.

◎ Aim here

The Campaign / Offer Configuration wizard — made effortless for a non-technical marketer.

It is where high configuration complexity (segments, triggers, benefits, validity, award rules) meets non-technical marketers who must self-serve without IT. It is also their flagship demo feature — and the exact flow both sides agreed as the trial task.

Won when: a marketer with no IT support can create a real offer end-to-end, the screen looks modern and enterprise-credible, and an advanced mode still holds the complex Shell cases. That is the client's stated success test, on the surface they show prospects.

  • Middle ring — usable by non-technical marketers. The client's repeated ask: "marketing people without IT support." The wizard is where that promise is kept or broken.

Three independent signals point to the same target: the client named it as their flagship feature [19:58], both sides agreed it as the trial task [39:51–39:53], and it's where the two forces they most want fixed — complexity + non-technical users — actually meet. The reframe to lead with

The wizard's information architecture is already good — a 5-step stepper (Target → Trigger → Validity → Benefit → Offer Details), a live Offer Summary rail, and an "Advanced Setup" escape hatch. So don't sell "we'll rebuild your flow." Sell "we'll make the flow you already have feel calm, modern, and self-explanatory" — restyling, interaction polish, plain language, and live feedback. Lower risk for them, faster for us, mindful of the Java + limited design system constraint.

02 Secondary pain points (address in the trial where cheap; name the rest)

HIGH

Red means everything → selected looks like an error

In the live tool, brand red is the CTA, the selected-card state, the validation border and the error colour. A chosen option renders in alarm-red. It makes a functional flow feel broken and stressful.

Evidence: screen inventory — every wizard step shows selected cards with heavy red border + pink fill (Target, Trigger, Benefit).

Split brand red (CTA/active only) from a calm "selected" treatment and a distinct semantic error. Biggest visible-calm win for the least effort — do it in the trial.

HIGH

No live audience feedback in the flow

You can't see how many customers a segment reaches until you click "Display Segment" (a modal / full stop). Marketers fly blind while targeting. Their own AI mockup already shows the fix — an "87,220 customers matching" breakdown — they just haven't built it in-flow.

Evidence: mockup_segment_breakdown.png vs the live Target step; competitor benchmark — Klaviyo/Braze show live segment size as you build.

Bring a live estimated-reach count inline into the Target step (sampled estimate at 9M scale). High "wow" in a demo — worth prototyping in the trial.

HIGH

Concepts are unclear even to Wonderline's own team

On the call, staff couldn't explain their own controls: "why do you need to select award role here? — it's a good question" [44:28]; validity vs price-validity confusion [45:55]; campaign vs "test campaign" [47:49]. If the makers hesitate, a marketer has no chance.

Evidence: transcript [44:28], [45:55], [47:49]. Escalate the domain truth to the business analyst on the next call.

Plain-language labels, inline helper text, and sensible defaults. Requires analyst input — flag as a dependency, resolve on 16 Sep.

MED

~50-item, jargon-heavy left navigation

The menu overwhelms: ~18 items under "Rewards" alone, with internal names like "C/V Definitions", "Beacon Map", "Engagewave", "CFlow". Alex: "there are so many different thing… it can be a little bit overwhelming" [37:16].

Evidence: screen inventory (nav enumeration); transcript [37:16–37:37].

Out of trial scope to fix fully, but show a grouped, plain-language nav concept as a "here's where this goes next" teaser. Signals the bigger redesign without over-committing.

MED

Simple vs advanced isn't cleanly separated

Complex Shell offers have many rules; they want power kept but not in the marketer's face: "keep it as an advanced mode… if anybody wants to go into details" [48:34].

Evidence: transcript [48:16–48:48]; the "Go to Advanced Setup" button already exists.

Design the basic ↔ advanced handoff explicitly (state carries over). Show the pattern in the trial; it's the differentiator vs Comarch's "powerful but complex" reputation.

MED

Cramped, unlabelled controls

Criteria + unit + product group + a bare gear icon pile into one tight row; expand-in-place sections feel dense. Small stuff, but it's the texture that reads "old."

Evidence: real_wizard_trigger_product.png, real_wizard_benefit.png.

Spacing, grouping, labelled affordances, consistent field sizing. Cheap, and it's what makes screenshots look modern.

03 How to present it on the next call

Open with the reframe, not the redesign. "Your flow is structurally sound — here's how we make it feel as capable as it is." That respects the work their team already did (they're sensitive about it) and lowers perceived risk.

Lead the demo with two moves that photograph well: (1) the calm colour system — selected no longer looks like an error; (2) live audience reach in the Target step. Both are instantly legible in a prospect demo.

Name the bigger prizes without scope-creeping the trial: the nav restructure and the basic/advanced handoff — as "the direction after the trial," aligned with the incoming rebrand.

Bring your open questions to the analyst (award rule, validity semantics, test vs real campaign). Asking sharp domain questions signals you take their complexity seriously — which is exactly the "simple but handles complex environments" story they're buying.
